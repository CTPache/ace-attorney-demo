## Incident Overview

On the evening of September 17th, Kurumi Nakaki, a stunt performer for the Steel Samurai show at Joypolis, was found dead on the 1st-floor stage of the atrium event hall. She had fallen from the 4th-floor railing. The body was discovered at 10:05 PM by event hall supervisor Frank Sahwit, who claimed to have witnessed security guard Maggey Byrde push the victim off the railing. Maggey, who could not clearly remember what happened due to having lost her glasses, was arrested and charged with murder.

---

## Background

### The Victim

Kurumi Nakaki was a deeply dedicated stunt performer who played the lead in the Joypolis Steel Samurai show. Her passion drove her to practice her routines inside the facility well past closing hours—a habit that made her a constant headache for the security staff. Despite the friction this caused, she was widely respected for her commitment to the craft.

### The Defendant

Maggey Byrde was a former police officer and Detective Gumshoe's junior at the local precinct. After leaving the force, she took a part-time job as a security guard at Joypolis. Maggey had a reputation for extraordinarily bad luck—trouble seemed to follow her wherever she went. As part of her duties, she repeatedly clashed with Nakaki over after-hours practice, though Maggey bore no personal ill will toward the performer.

Maggey suffered from severe astigmatism and was functionally blind without her glasses. She maintained a personal blog—*"The Jinxed Fallen Angel's Blog"*—where she posted photos of her prized glasses charms.

### The True Culprit

Frank Sahwit was the event hall supervisor at Joypolis. He presented himself as a concerned, mild-mannered manager who tried to mediate between Byrde and Nakaki. In reality, he had asked Nakaki out and was furiously rejected. The murder was his revenge.

Sahwit was widely suspected (though never confirmed) of wearing a toupee, a running gag throughout the case. Detective Gumshoe bluntly noted that his "hair" never stood on end despite his claims of terror, and Phoenix repeatedly commented on its "unnatural twitching."

## Investigation

Phoenix Wright investigated four key locations:

### Joypolis Entrance — Intro Briefing
- Gumshoe summarized the case: victim Kurumi Nakaki, stunt performer, body discovered at 10:05 PM, estimated time of death between 9:30 PM and 10:30 PM.
- Edgeworth declared "decisive evidence" already existed pointing to Byrde's guilt.
- Phoenix learned that Maggey was Gumshoe's former junior at the police force, and that the detective was personally invested in her innocence.

### Event Hall — Crime Scene (1st Floor)
- The victim's body lay **face-down** on the 1st-floor stage, still wearing the full Steel Samurai suit and mask. Blood seeped through gaps in the mask.
- **Body position contradiction:** Phoenix noted that if the fatal blow was to the *back* of the head, the body should be lying face-*up*. Gumshoe confirmed injuries on both the face and the back of the head.
- The atrium had been checked: no traps, gimmicks, or structural tricks—confirming a straightforward fall.
- The facility was closed at 9:00 PM; no guests were present. Only Sahwit, Nakaki, and Byrde were in the building.
- The floor was clean—nothing else fell with the body.
- After completing the examination, Sahwit appeared and made dramatic remarks about the trauma of discovering the body. He claimed "every hair on my body is standing straight up," prompting Gumshoe to observe that his hair wasn't standing up at all (implying a toupee).
- After Sahwit departed, Phoenix found a **glasses charm accessory** on the floor where Sahwit had been standing.

### Office — Frank Sahwit's Account
- Sahwit described Nakaki as devoted to the show, practicing late every night.
- He confirmed the dispute between Byrde and Nakaki and claimed he had arranged a 10:00 PM mediation meeting on the 4th floor.
- He testified that he waited on the 1st-floor stage from about 9:15 PM onwards and heard the two women arguing from above before seeing Byrde push Nakaki off the 4th floor.
- When Phoenix presented the "Loud Sound" evidence, Sahwit acknowledged hearing a loud noise—but claimed it was only the 10:05 PM fall. Phoenix noted the time discrepancy with Byrde's account (~9:30 PM).

### Detention Center — Maggey Byrde's Account
- Byrde described her routine: she argued briefly with Nakaki about after-hours practice, then completed the security log and updated her blog with a photo of her glasses charms at **9:27 PM**.
- Shortly after, she showered as requested by Sahwit. When she got out, **her glasses were gone**. The pair she wore in court were spares brought from home.
- Without glasses, she could barely see. She stumbled to the 4th-floor railing in darkness, saw a faint shape, called out with no response, and reached out. She heard a crash below.
- Around **9:30 PM**, while in the shower, she heard a **loud impact sound** from outside—audible even over the running water.

### 4th Floor — Gumshoe's Insights
- Gumshoe expressed his conviction that Maggey was innocent, sharing his personal history of wanting to be a stunt performer.
- He presented a **photo of the Steel Samurai mask** worn by the victim. The eyebrow area was slightly dented, but the rest of the mask—including the back and the iconic topknot—had **no scratches or damage whatsoever**.
- Gumshoe interpreted this as the victim "protecting the topknot with her life" while falling. Phoenix privately disagreed, recognizing it as a critical clue.

## Trial — September 18th, District Court No. 2

### Pre-Trial Lobby

Maya Fey joined Phoenix as co-counsel. Maggey expressed anxiety; Phoenix reassured her. Maya teased Edgeworth about wanting to rush the trial so he could go play at Joypolis—a running joke throughout the proceedings.

### Opening

Edgeworth presented the prosecution's case: Byrde and Nakaki had a well-known antagonistic relationship. Byrde had effectively admitted to pushing the victim. The fall occurred in front of an eyewitness (Sahwit), and the photo evidence confirmed it.

Phoenix objected that Byrde never explicitly confessed—she merely speculated that she *might* have accidentally pushed someone.

### Witness Testimony 1 — "The Situation at the Time of the Incident"

Sahwit testified:
1. He called both women to the 4th floor for a 10:00 PM mediation meeting.
2. He waited on the 1st-floor stage from ~9:15 PM.
3. He looked up and saw the victim and defendant on the 4th floor.
4. The defendant "approached the victim without any hesitation and pushed her right off."

**Cross-Examination — The Missing Glasses:**
Phoenix presented the **Blog Post — Glasses Charm** evidence. Byrde's glasses had gone missing around 9:30 PM, and she had severe astigmatism. How could someone who was functionally blind "approach the victim without any hesitation"? There was a chance she could have fallen on her own—making the push implausible.

The Judge acknowledged the point. Edgeworth countered by demanding proof that the glasses were actually lost (claiming it was only Byrde's word). He also argued that the witness testimony and photos showed Byrde walking normally, meaning she must have been wearing glasses.

Byrde insisted her glasses were genuinely stolen and her spares were at home. She testified that she could barely see, saw only a faint shadow, called out, reached her hand, and then heard something crash below.

Phoenix, cornered, desperately claimed Byrde might have "dropped something else"—Edgeworth mockingly dismissed this. But Edgeworth then revealed he had **photographs** taken by Sahwit at the time of the incident.

### Witness Testimony 2 — "Photo of the Falling Victim"

Sahwit presented a sequence of cell phone photos:
1. The defendant's legs are visible as she stands near the railing.
2. The defendant is pushing the victim as she sits on the railing.
3. The victim falls face-first.
4. (Added after pressing) The victim flipped over on impact, and the blow to the back of the head came from the second impact.

**Cross-Examination — The Mask Contradiction:**
Phoenix presented the **Mask Evidence Photo**. If the victim flipped over and struck the back of her head, the Steel Samurai mask's topknot should be broken and the back should be scratched. But the topknot was completely intact and the back of the mask was undamaged—only the eyebrow area was dented from the face-first impact.

This proved the victim **was not wearing the mask** when she received the fatal blow to the back of her head.

**The "Fell Twice" Deduction:**
Phoenix proposed that the victim fell **twice**:
1. **First fall (~9:30 PM):** Without the mask. This is when the victim received the fatal cerebral contusion to the back of the head. This fall produced the loud sound Byrde heard while showering.
2. **Second fall (~10:05 PM):** With the mask. The already-dead victim was propped on the railing and toppled face-first when Byrde reached out. Only the eyebrow area dented because the mask absorbed the frontal impact; the back of the mask stayed pristine because the victim was already dead and didn't flip.

Phoenix argued that since Byrde was showering at 9:30 PM, she couldn't have committed the murder. The real killer was someone else.

Edgeworth demanded proof and challenged the assumption of two falls. The Judge asked why Phoenix concluded the victim was specifically "dropped" rather than killed by other means. Phoenix requested a third cross-examination of Sahwit, focusing on his unaccounted **50 minutes** between 9:15 PM and the incident.

### Witness Testimony 3 — "The Blank 50 Minutes"

Sahwit testified:
1. He finished work around 9:15 PM and stayed on the 1st-floor stage the entire time.
2. He denied any involvement and claimed to have never gone to the 4th floor.
3. He stated there was "not a single strange occurrence" during the entire period.

**Cross-Examination — The Loud Sound:**
Phoenix presented the **Loud Sound** evidence. Byrde heard a loud impact at ~9:30 PM—audible even over a running shower on the 4th floor. Sahwit, sitting on the 1st floor with nothing to muffle it, claimed he didn't hear it because he was "deep in thought." Phoenix declared this impossible.

Sahwit backtracked, admitting he heard a loud noise—but claimed it was only the 10:05 PM fall. Edgeworth supported this, arguing the earlier noise was merely Byrde's unsupported claim.

**The Photo Revelation — Missing Face:**
Maya directed Phoenix's attention back to Sahwit's photos. The defendant's face was never visible in any of them—only her legs. Phoenix deduced:
- Byrde wasn't wearing her glasses at the time the photos were taken.
- If her face were shown, the absence of glasses would be obvious.
- Sahwit **deliberately** framed the photos to hide this fact.

Edgeworth countered by submitting Byrde's actual glasses as new evidence—found in the waiting room during the post-incident search. He argued Byrde lied about losing them to disguise a deliberate murder as an accident.

### The Breakthrough — The Accessory

Phoenix presented the **Accessory** (glasses charm) found at the crime scene—dropped by Sahwit during the investigation. He connected it to Byrde's blog post photo, which showed **two** charms attached to her glasses. The glasses submitted by Edgeworth only had **one** charm. The missing charm was the one Sahwit dropped.

Sahwit exploded in anger, admitting he dropped the charm at the murder scene but claiming he "found it at the amusement park entrance on the morning of the incident." Phoenix pressed him to confirm this claim—then demanded the court examine the blog post.

### The Final Proof

Phoenix presented the **Blog Post — Glasses Charm**, uploaded at **9:27 PM on September 17th**—the night of the incident. The photo clearly showed both charms attached. It was impossible for Sahwit to have "found" one of them that morning, because both charms were still on the glasses at 9:27 PM that night. He could only have obtained it by handling the glasses after 9:27 PM.

Phoenix offered to request a fingerprint analysis on the charm—and predicted Byrde's fingerprints would be found on it.

Sahwit suffered a dramatic breakdown, throwing his toupee at Phoenix before collapsing.

### Reconstruction

Phoenix laid out the full sequence:
1. Sahwit went up to the 4th floor around 9:30 PM, confronted the practicing Nakaki, and pushed her over the railing. She fell to the 1st floor and died instantly from the impact to the back of her head.
2. The loud noise from this fall reached Byrde's ears in the shower.
3. Sahwit carried the victim's body back up to the 4th floor, put the Steel Samurai mask on her, and propped her on the railing.
4. He sneaked into the waiting room and stole Byrde's glasses while she was showering—causing one glasses charm to detach. He pocketed it unknowingly.
5. He calculated that Byrde, now blind, would stumble to the railing and inadvertently knock the body off—making her appear to be the killer.
6. He returned to the 1st floor and waited. At 10:05 PM, everything played out as planned.
7. During the crime scene investigation, he attempted to return the glasses to the waiting room, but dropped the charm at the murder scene—where Phoenix found it.

---

## Motive and Confession

Sahwit confessed that he had asked Kurumi Nakaki to go out with him, and she flatly rejected him. Enraged that his "special treatment" and favoritism had gone unreciprocated, he murdered her in a fit of fury and devised the elaborate frame-up of Maggey Byrde.

---

## Verdict

**Maggey Byrde — Not Guilty.**

## Epilogue

In the courthouse lobby, Maggey thanked Phoenix but expressed guilt over having inadvertently knocked Nakaki's body off the railing. Phoenix reassured her that the victim was already dead by then. Maya revealed that Gumshoe had volunteered to play the Steel Samurai in a last-minute show to celebrate Maggey's acquittal. Phoenix wondered aloud whether Gumshoe could even fit into the suit.
