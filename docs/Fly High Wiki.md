'''''Turnabout Fly High''''' is a case from the Joypolis ''Ace Attorney'' attraction. It features [[Phoenix Wright]] defending former police officer [[Maggey Byrde]] after she is accused of pushing a stunt performer off a balcony at Joypolis.

==Sept. 17==
=== The crime ===
At around 9:30 p.m., Joypolis event hall supervisor [[Frank Sahwit]] went up to the fourth floor of the atrium and confronted [[Kurumi Nakaki]], a dedicated stunt performer for the Steel Samurai show who had rejected his romantic advances. In a fit of furious revenge, he pushed her off the railing. She fell to the first-floor stage and died instantly from a cerebral contusion to the back of the head.

Knowing that part-time security guard [[Maggey Byrde]] was taking a shower nearby, Sahwit carried Nakaki's body back up to the fourth floor, put the Steel Samurai mask on her, and propped her body on the railing in a seated position. He then snuck into the shower's waiting room and stole Byrde's glasses, unknowingly dropping one of her glasses' custom charms at the crime scene in the process.

===Arrest===
At 10:05 p.m., a functionally blind Byrde stumbled out of the shower room and approached the 4th-floor railing for a 10:00 p.m. mediation meeting Sahwit had previously scheduled. Seeing a vague silhouette, she called out, got no response, and reached out her hand. The already-dead victim toppled forward, falling face-first out of the mask and onto the 1st floor. Sahwit, waiting below on the 1st floor, took photos of Byrde "pushing" the victim and began screaming for help.

Byrde was swiftly arrested as the sole suspect, unable to clearly defend herself due to her poor eyesight at the time. 

===Investigation===
[[Phoenix Wright]] arrived at Joypolis at 10:34 p.m. to investigate alongside [[Dick Gumshoe]] and prosecuting attorney [[Miles Edgeworth]]. 

Wright investigated the crime scene at the Joypolis Atrium Event Hall. On the 1st-floor stage, he examined Nakaki's body, which lay face-down in her Steel Samurai suit. Wright noted a discrepancy: the fatal blow was to the back of the head, yet the body was face-down. Sahwit met them there, claiming to be terrified, though Gumshoe dryly noted his hair (suspected to be a toupee) wasn't standing on end. After Sahwit departed, Wright recovered a dropped glasses charm accessory from where the manager had been standing.

In the office, Sahwit claimed to have waited on the first floor from 9:15 p.m. onwards and witnessed Byrde push Nakaki. 

At the Detention Center, Wright spoke with a despondent Byrde. She explained that she had spent about 30 minutes writing her security log before uploading a post to her "Jinxed Fallen Angel's Blog" showing off her favorite glasses charms at 9:27 p.m. Shortly after, she remembered Sahwit had called a 10:00 p.m. meeting, so she hurried to take a shower. Around 9:30 p.m., while showering, she heard a remarkably loud impact. Exiting the shower, she discovered her glasses had been stolen. Functionally blind without them, she cautiously approached the 4th-floor railing, saw a shadowy figure, and reached out—only to hear something fall to the floor below.

Up on the 4th floor, Gumshoe lamented Byrde's famously terrible luck. Reminiscing about his own past dreams of becoming a suit actor, Gumshoe shared a "top secret" piece of evidence: a photo of the victim's Steel Samurai mask. He proudly pointed out that while the eyebrow area was dented, the back and iconic topknot were completely undamaged. Gumshoe absurdly theorized that the victim "protected the topknot with her life" as she fell. Wright privately dismissed the detective's theory but realized the untouched topknot provided the critical contradiction needed to prove Byrde's innocence.

==Sept. 18==
===Trial ===
The trial began in District Court No. 2 with [[Miles Edgeworth]] leading the prosecution. He called [[Frank Sahwit]] to the stand, who testified that he witnessed Byrde approach Nakaki without hesitation and push her off the 4th floor. Wright countered this by presenting Byrde's blog post, uploaded at 9:27 p.m. the previous night, arguing that Byrde's glasses had gone missing shortly after, making it impossible for her to act without hesitation.

Sahwit presented photographs showing Byrde's legs as she seemingly pushed the victim, who then fell face-first. Sahwit claimed the victim flipped over on impact to explain the fatal blow to the back of the head. However, Wright presented a photo of the Steel Samurai mask the victim wore, showing damage only to the eyebrow area and leaving the iconic topknot perfectly intact. Wright deduced that the victim fell ''twice'': once without the mask at 9:30 p.m. (the real murder), and once with the mask at 10:05 p.m. (a setup involving an already dead body).

The final blow came when Wright tied the glasses accessory found at the crime scene to Byrde's glasses. The prosecution had submitted Byrde's recovered glasses as evidence, but they only had one attached charm. Byrde's 9:27 p.m. blog post photo proved both charms were attached right before her shower. Since Sahwit dropped the missing charm at the murder scene, it proved he had handled her glasses after 9:27 p.m., placing him at the scene of the crime and definitively linking him to the theft. 

Cornered and facing a fingerprint analysis on the charm, Sahwit suffered a breakdown, throwing his toupee at Wright and confessing to the murder. Maggey Byrde was declared Not Guilty.

===Aftermath ===
In the lobby, Byrde expressed guilt over knocking the body off the balcony, though Wright assured her Nakaki was already dead. In celebration of her acquittal, Detective Gumshoe volunteered to play the Steel Samurai in a last-minute show, leaving Phoenix to wonder if Gumshoe could even fit into the suit.

== Miscellaneous ==
* [[Frank Sahwit]]'s toupee becomes a running joke; Gumshoe notes that his hair never stands on end despite his claims of terror, and Wright repeatedly mentions its unnatural twitching.
* [[Maya Fey]] frequently teases [[Miles Edgeworth]] during the proceedings, joking that he wants to rush the trial so he can go play at Joypolis.
* This case is rare in that the culprit drops the victim's dead body to stage the time of death and frame the defendant.
